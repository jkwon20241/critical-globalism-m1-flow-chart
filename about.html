About this project

Jennie Kwon

Why a website

The assignment asks for a map that is “easily accessible” and that replaces written transitions with illustrations. As a graphic design student, I treated that as a design brief: a website opens anywhere, and it lets the reader follow one author down the page or compare all three texts side by side, which matches how the readings answer the same questions from different positions.

The visual system

I borrowed the conventions of the flowchart and gave each text one simple shape and one color, so the system can be read by shape alone. A narrow type hierarchy, click-to-open shapes, and a contents sidebar keep the first view calm for a reader outside of design, while the full argument stays one click away.

Lowe Wynter Koshy et al. Question Example

Design and the course

Flowchart symbols are set by an international standard (ISO 5807), which presents them as neutral and universal. Reading Wynter on the overrepresentation of Man, and Harris’s claim that “the racelessness of the object is illusory” (90), made me see that design conventions also decide whom they are built for. Thus, I used the flowchart knowingly: as a familiar structure that makes the readings accessible, and as a reminder that every visual system, like every definition of the human, includes some and leaves others out.
